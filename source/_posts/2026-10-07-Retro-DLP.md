## YouTube on Retro Apple Devices

There are so many challenges with playing back YouTube videos on retro Apple
devices. I covered this in detail in my 
[Retro Stream Tutorial]({% post_url 2025-08-17-Retro-Stream-Tutorial %}) so 
I won't go through everything here, but the short story is... H.264 is very 
difficult to playback without a dedicated decoder, like iPhones have. The TLS 
Apocalypse means the device likely can't connect at all. YouTube relies on 
modern Javascript to obscure the URL's where the videos can be fetched. Since 
retro devices are bad at all of these things, I needed to get creative.

## What is Retro-DLP

Retro-DLP is a minimal reimplementation of of yt-dlp in C, with a CLI and native 
apps for iOS 5+ and Mac OS X Tiger+. I did my best to copy the CLI of yt-dlp,
so if you are familiar with that, hopefully you will be familiar with Retro-DLP.

Why did I choose yt-dlp as the base? Well, I like this tool, I like the way
it works, and I like the options it gives you. It lets you download the videos 
in the quality you want and stitch them together. It supports authenticating 
with your Youtube Cookies from your browser. As well, yt-dlp supports to 
fetching playlists.

So I basically re-implemented these features.

## How Retro-DLP Works

As with all of these projects, I stand on the shoulders of giants. And in this
case, its a super giant because I am using
[QuickJS](https://bellard.org/quickjs/) by Fabrice Bellard which is an
ECMAScript compliant JavaScript runtime written entirely in C that runs on
devices with very tight memory requirements like retro computers. Fabrice
Bellard also wrote FFMPEG and QEMU which are basically the technolies that run
YouTube and AWS to this day. So yeah... big giants with this project.

Retro-DLP follows the same path as yt-dlp to download videos, it just does it
with different technologies:

1. Connect to Youtube and fetch the Player Javascript (libcurl + OpenSSL)
1. Fetch special EJS Solver JavaScript (libcurl + OpenSSL + yt-dlp)
1. Solve the JavaScript challenges presented by YouTube (QuickJS)
1. Fetch the Audio and Video files from Youtube (libcurl + OpenSSL)
1. Mux together the Audio and Video files into a single MP4 (L-SMASH)

This is my favorite kind of integration project. Take well known, battle tested
C libraries and bundle them all together to solve problems in a unique way.
The fact that this can run on a PowerPC Mac running Mac OS X Tiger from 2004
is mindblowing. And of course if you have a fast internet connection, the 
slowest part of the operation is the JavaScript part... of course... JavaScript
being shit as always.

## How I Use Retro-DLP

### Longform Content

I like to watch a bit of a YouTube video while I eat lunch. Sounds boring, but
its how I take a break from conversation. To find videos to download with 
Retro-DLP, I use RSS-THE-PLANET which provides my YouTube playlists and
Subscriptions new videos as an RSS feed. From there I can copy the video URL
into Retro-DLP and watch it.

### K-POP Stan (Shortform Content)

I love K-POP and around December 2025 as I was preparing for the year of 
unenshittification, I got iTunes on my iMac G4 to sync with my iPhone 5 
(which is officially unsupported) and then I started bulk buying used CD's from
my favorite groups and ripping them 1by1 in iTunes like it was 2003. It was
awesome. So this synced music in the Music app on my iPhone 5 represents the 
bulk of my music listening. However, K-POP moves fast. And new songs and
videos are being released all the time. So I have a playlist in YouTube to keep
track of these before I buy the CD and import them into my iMac G4. 
Using Retro-DLP's playlist, I keep a small selection of new tracks that are 
missing from the Music app.

## Custom Video Player

On Mac OS X, Retro-DLP does not have a video player. Its a Mac and so instead,
Retro-DLP maintains playlist files that work in VLC. So any video or any 
playlist can easily be played in VLC and it "Just Works"

Of course on iOS, its not so easy. I made the decision to make Retro-DLP
a normal sandboxed iOS app distributed as an IPA even though I knew it would
mean I could not play the video is VLC which is what I really wanted. But
its just so much easier for people to install IPA's... hell, even iTunes
will do it for you. So I went with that path. But that meant I needed to build
a custom video player in iOS which is something I never wanted to do because
they are actually fiendishly complex.

I spent a lot of time making the custom video player in the iOS app really 
nice and I tried to hide the complexity so hopefully no one would notice 
how much functionality it has built in. Complexity which was required because
of the need to support foreground and background playback, long-form content, 
short-form content, and playslists. Of course it does all of this with
AVFoundation but not AVPlayerViewController because that was introduced in
iOS 8 and I target iOS 5.

## The Annoying Parts

This does not magically make an iMac G4 good at decoding HD H.264. I already
wrote about that in my [Retro Stream Tutorial]({% post_url 2025-08-17-Retro-Stream-Tutorial %}).
You still have to choose a quality your device can play.

And solving the JavaScript challenges does not solve every YouTube restriction.
Retro-DLP doesn't generate Proof of Origin tokens, so higher-quality downloads
can fail. The README covers this, cookies, and the other limitations.

## Appendix

### Existing Solutions

I should call out that for iPhones, there there is 
[TubeRepair](https://www.reddit.com/r/LegacyJailbreak/comments/1oggj03/ios_6_youtube_fix_tutorial/) 
which keeps the original Apple YouTube app alive. This solution involves someone
hosting a server that replicates the REST API that the Apple app originally
relied on and proxying connections from that app to this custom server. It works
OK but its iPhone only and its not super reliable as its a hobby project. Plus
it does not work offline because it has the original limitations of the old app.
Also, you can't or definitely should not login because that would pass your
Google credentials to an unknown person. So no playlists or recommendations.

### Why Is Downloading YouTube Videos So Hard

You might think downloading a video means finding a URL and downloading it.
And yes, eventually. But first there is JavaScript homework.

YouTube's web player doesn't just hand you a ready-to-use video URL.
Parts of the URL need to be transformed by functions buried in the player's
JavaScript. YouTube supplies both the puzzle and the code to solve it, but
you still need a working modern JavaScript engine to run that code. Good luck
asking the Safari that came with Tiger to do that.

So even if the device can play the video, getting a usable download URL
can be the thing that stops you. Apparently downloading an MP4 requires
bringing part of a modern browser along for the ride.
